About the Studio

MEHRAD
& ANITA

Two Oxford scientists. A shared belief that the world's most fascinating chemistry belongs on the table — not just in textbooks.

We are Catalyst Board Game Design, founded in Oxford by Mehrad Niasari — chemist and researcher at Oxford University — and Anita Mansouri — statistician at AstraZeneca.

Mehrad synthesises the chemistry into mechanics. Anita optimises the systems, stress-tests every interaction, and ensures every game is as playable as it is scientifically rigorous. Together we've built three titles that sit at the intersection of science, strategy, and story.

We also believe these games belong in classrooms. Every Catalyst title is a gateway — a way to show students that chemistry is one of the most creative, dramatic, and surprising disciplines that exists.

"A great game is a working model of something true — a system that teaches by letting you fail, adapt, and discover."
MN
Mehrad Niasari
Game Designer · Chemist
Oxford University · Chemistry & Biochemistry

Mehrad is the scientific engine of Catalyst. His background in organic chemistry and sustainable materials drives every mechanic — from authentic distillation in Jabir to real polymer science in Synthelyst. He has worked in bioplastics R&D and understands the commercial, regulatory, and scientific landscape his games portray.

AM
Anita Mansouri
Game Designer · Statistician
AstraZeneca · Data Science & Optimisation

Anita brings statistical precision to game design. She runs balance analyses, optimises player experience across all game states, and ensures every Catalyst game is deeply replayable. She is also an experienced board game player — the critical eye that ensures scientific accuracy never comes at the cost of fun.

Our Design Philosophy

Games where you win by understanding — not just by optimising.

Every Catalyst title rests on one conviction: the deepest fun comes from the moment a system clicks in your mind. When you realise why silk must be washed cold — because protein fibres denature above 30°C — the rule stops being arbitrary. It becomes knowledge. That's the reward.

We also believe our games belong in schools. Science motivation disguised as an excellent evening of strategy.
